FinLens Review
I've read the handoff, the campaign log, the roadmap, and the audit closely. Here's the assessment. I'll start with D because you're right that it's the load-bearing question, and the answer reframes A, B, and C.
Lens D — Moat vs. Commercial Product
The honest answer: you are building a third thing that neither document admits, and the frame is letting you avoid noticing.
Let me be precise about what the evidence shows. Your commits are ~90% data-moat work. Your roadmap is 100% feature-SaaS. Your stated frame is "best personal tool for me first." And your handoff reveals the detail that detonates all three: you hold no public equities. Capital is in a private VC fund and private former-employer shares. You can't dogfood a public-market value-investing tool because you have no public-market positions to analyze.
So "best personal value-investing tool for me first" cannot be literally true right now. There is no "me first" use happening. What you're actually doing is building a Canadian IFRS extraction-and-validation pipeline, and the value-investing-tool framing is the story layered on top. That's not a criticism of the work — the work is genuinely good and unusually disciplined. It's a criticism of the frame, because the frame is hiding the decision you keep not making.
Three products are tangled here, and they have different customers, different timelines, and barely overlapping build orders:

A retail value-investing SaaS (the roadmap). Customer: individual investors, finance students. The features are mostly not started.
A Canadian fundamentals data asset (the commits). Customer: fund managers, fintechs, academics — B2B, via API or licensing, not a $49/mo consumer app.
A skill demonstration for a VC career (the meta-goal). "Customer" is a future GP reading your work.

These are not phases of one product. They pull in opposite directions. Product 1 needs the Filing Diff Viewer, the memo builder, Catalyst Calendar — user-facing surface. Product 2 needs coverage depth, extraction reliability, and a sellable API — and none of the 35 roadmap features advance it. Product 3 is best served by whichever of 1 or 2 you can show working end-to-end, which right now is neither, because production is 194 commits behind and misreports financial magnitudes.
Is the moat real?
Partially, and smaller than the roadmap claims. Let me steelman it and then puncture it.
The steelman: SEDAR+ is PDF-only with no XBRL. If you build the only validated machine-readable Canadian IFRS fundamentals dataset, and every filing compounds the alias/sector-rule corpus, you have something a US-built competitor won't bother to replicate, and you can sell it to Canadian buy-side.
The punctures:

"Only validated dataset in existence" is almost certainly false. S&P Capital IQ, FactSet, Refinitiv, Morningstar, and Bloomberg all carry Canadian fundamentals. They standardize IFRS filings at scale. Your differentiator isn't "the only dataset" — it's "free/cheap, with a transparent audit trail, Canadian-focused." That's a real but much narrower wedge. The roadmap's framing ("the only validated Canadian fundamentals dataset in existence") is the kind of claim that feels true at 13 filers and gets you laughed out of a fund manager's office. Kill that sentence.
13 filers is not a corpus; it's a proof of concept. A Canadian fund manager covers hundreds of names. The TSX has ~1,500 listed issuers. Your moat thesis requires coverage that is years of manual-download-plus-extract away at the current rate — and your own log says the fetch path is Riley-manually-downloading-PDFs-in-a-browser because SEDAR+ is Radware-CAPTCHA-blocked and you have no API. That is not a flywheel. A flywheel doesn't require you to personally hand-crank every revolution. The "error-correction flywheel" language describes the alias-learning loop, which is real and compounding — but it's bottlenecked behind a manual acquisition step that doesn't scale. The compounding asset is gated by a non-compounding input.
The moat compounds on the wrong axis for a consumer SaaS. Every hour on insurance_sector IFRS 17 mapping makes the data better. It does nothing for the thing that converts a $49/mo subscriber: can they look up a company and get a decision-useful answer. Your paying retail user does not care that SLF's "Total net income (loss)" got promoted to a default alias. They care whether the app tells them if Sun Life is cheap.

If the moat is real, who pays and when?
The B2B data-API buyer (Canadian fund managers/fintechs) is a legitimate business but it is the slowest possible path to revenue, requires sales motion you have zero evidence of building, requires coverage you're years from, and requires reliability guarantees ("validated" means someone is liable when it's wrong). That's a post-2027, post-funding business. It is not a path to $5–10k MRR this year. If you're honest that this is the real product, then the roadmap, the pricing tiers, and the curriculum are all decoration and should be shelved, not "diverged from."
The verdict on the frame
"Best personal tool for me first, dogfooding deferred to September" is not coherent — it's an avoidance structure. Here's the tell: it's unfalsifiable until September. You've built a frame where no user feedback, no revenue, and no usage can challenge the direction for three more months, and the one person who's supposed to validate it (you) can't, because you have nothing to analyze. That's not a strategy; it's a permission slip to keep doing the satisfying engineering work — the extraction puzzles, the sector profiles, the clean test suites — without ever facing a market.
And the engineering is satisfying. That's the trap. SEDAR extraction is a beautifully-scoped technical problem with crisp success criteria (does it map? do tests pass? is the audit trail clean?). Commercializing is an ugly, ambiguous problem with no green checkmark. You are, with great discipline, choosing the tractable problem over the important one and calling it moat construction.

Lens C — Blind Spots
Most likely reason FinLens fails to commercialize: You never ship to a single real user, because every quarter has a satisfying-but-non-commercial reason to keep building infrastructure. The pattern is already visible: production is 194 commits and months behind; the consumer features are 1-of-35 done; the work that did happen is the work with the cleanest technical feedback loop. This is not a resourcing problem or a market problem. It's a gravitational pull toward tractable engineering over ambiguous go-to-market, and you have unusually good tooling (the three-way pipeline, the test gates, the audit cadence) that makes the tractable work even more rewarding and frictionless — which makes the pull stronger, not weaker. Your process discipline is, perversely, an accelerant for the failure mode.
What you're systematically underweighting:

Distribution. There is not one line in any document about how a stranger discovers FinLens. Zero. For a solo founder, distribution is the entire game and it's absent from a 487-line roadmap.
Liability of "validated." The moment you sell "validated data," a wrong number is your fault. Your own integrity-first principle ("honest skip > fabricated value") is good engineering ethics but a commercial landmine: a data customer wants coverage and a number, and "we honestly skipped this field" is a product gap to them, not a virtue.
The competitive reality of the consumer tier. Finchat/Koyfin/Morningstar exist, are funded, and ship fast. The roadmap names them but doesn't reckon with the fact that a solo dev shipping 1 consumer feature per several months cannot out-feature them. The only defensible consumer angle is SEDAR+ depth — which loops back to the data product, which isn't a consumer play.

Where you're most deceiving yourself about which product you're building: You believe you're building a value-investing SaaS that happens to have a strong data backend. The evidence says you're building a Canadian fundamentals data pipeline that has a value-investing-SaaS story attached for narrative coherence (and for the VC-career optionality). The September dogfooding deferral and the no-public-equities fact together mean you have arranged things so that you never have to find out which one is true. That's the core self-deception. Not "moat vs. SaaS" — it's that the frame is engineered to be unfalsifiable.
