# How Bank Research Analysts Analyze Bank Filings — and Where FinLens's Coverage Stands

**Research report — 2026-07-11**

This report is the third in the sector-coverage series, after `docs/research/2026-07-10-bank-og-coverage-gaps.md` (oil & gas) and `docs/research/2026-07-11-bank-mining-coverage-gaps.md` (mining). The ask: reconstruct how sell-side FIG desks (RBC, TD, BMO, Scotia, CIBC, National Bank Financial, Canaccord) actually value and monitor the Canadian Big Six, then map every load-bearing metric to the document class it lives in — and to what FinLens ingests today. Method: a four-angle sweep (valuation, margin/revenue, credit, capital) across sell-side/FIG valuation references, OSFI regulatory texts (CAR Guideline, DSB decisions, TLAC/NSFR Disclosure Guidelines, the 2022 Assurance Guideline), the Basel Pillar 3 framework, IFRS 7/9/15 and IAS 1, and the primary filings of real Big Six issuers; each claim was then adversarially verified — with the **source-document attribution attacked hardest** — against issuer disclosures. Tally: **21 confirmed, 3 refuted (corrected below), 0 unverified.**

Primary sources verified against (real filers): **RBC** FY2024/FY2025 Annual Report + MD&A, Q1/Q2/Q4 2026 and Q2–Q4 2025 Supplementary Financial Information (SFI) packages, standalone Q1/Q4 2025–2026 Pillar 3 Reports, 40-F/6-K exhibits (SEC CIK 1000275), quarterly earnings releases; **TD** Q1/Q4 2026 and FY2024/FY2025 6-K/earnings releases + Supplemental Regulatory Disclosure (CIK 947263); **BMO** Q2/Q4 2025–2026 supplementary + FY2024 6-K (CIK 927971); **CIBC** FY2023/FY2025 Annual Report MD&A + audited statements (40-F ex-99, CIK 1045520); **Scotiabank** FY2024 40-F audited statements + MD&A; **National Bank** SFI. Regulator sources: OSFI DSB decisions (June/Dec 2025, June 2026), CAR Guideline, TLAC Disclosure Requirements Guideline (2018) + per-bank TLAC Orders, NSFR Disclosure Guideline, Assurance on Capital/Leverage/Liquidity Returns Guideline (2022); BIS Basel III leverage framework. Non-issuer references: Wall Street Prep, BankSift/FIG-IB, the Bayes/City "How Do Equity Research Analysts Value Banks" study, AEI "Due North," Rosenberg Research, Canaccord, Simply Wall St, Globe & Mail analyst notes.

## The headline (confirmed)

The gap structure rhymes with O&G and mining — **it is about SOURCE DOCUMENTS, not metric cleverness** — but banking's document geometry is different in a way that matters for the build. Two things are true at once:

1. **Banking's credit and revenue-mix layer is far more statements/notes-resident than O&G or mining.** The single most valuable bank credit disclosure — the **IFRS 9 stage-migration reconciliation** (transfers into Stage 1/2/3, the earliest quantified deterioration signal) — is in the **audited notes** (finding C2, a *refuted* over-claim that actually improves the picture). Headline **PCL is one audited income-statement line** (finding 12); the **allowance for credit losses nets into the audited balance sheet** with a mandatory stage reconciliation note (finding 14); **gross impaired-loan dollars sit in the audited note** (finding 15); **write-offs are an audited reconciling line** (finding 16); **segment revenue and net income are in the audited IFRS 8 note** (finding 11); and — decisively — the **entire non-interest-income product mix is itemized on the face of the audited income statement** for Canadian Big Six banks (finding C1, refuted). None of this has an analog in O&G/mining, where AISC, netback, and production are *structurally absent* from the statements. **This is a large, receipted, cheap-win surface that FinLens maps zero of today.**

2. **But banking adds TWO new regulatory document classes on top of the MD&A** that have no O&G/mining equivalent in depth. Capital adequacy (CET1, Tier 1, Total capital, RWA composition, leverage exposure) lives in the **Supplementary Financial Information (SFI)** package and the standalone **Pillar 3 report** (findings 3, 4, 17, 18, 19); liquidity (LCR, NSFR) and loss-absorbing capacity (TLAC) live in the **MD&A + Pillar 3** with issuer-discretionary placement (findings 20, 21). The audited statements carry only the *headline ratio* (as a summary in the capital-management note) — never the capital stack, the RWA breakdown, the leverage reconciliation, or the liquidity templates that explain it. This is the direct analog of O&G's AIF / mining's NI 43-101, but **broader** (three regulatory documents, not one) and — critically — **standardized**: the Basel Pillar 3 templates (KM1, CC1/CC2, OV1, CR8, LR1/LR2, LIQ1/LIQ2, KM2/TLAC) are identical across the Big Six, and RBC posts its SFI in **Excel** as well as PDF. Unlike the bespoke, free-text NI 43-101 technical report, the bank regulatory layer is a *machine-readable form-parsing* target.

So the hypothesis — **is banking richer in statements-resident metrics? — is half-right, and the correct answer is load-bearing for the build order.** Banking's *credit-quality and revenue-mix* analysis is substantially more audited/receipted than O&G or mining (a genuine Bucket-A goldmine FinLens is entirely missing). But banking's *valuation, margin, and capital* analysis introduces an MD&A layer (ROTE, adjusted EPS, segment NIM, the NIM denominator, payout targets) **plus** two regulatory document classes (SFI, Pillar 3) — so the total document surface is wider than O&G/mining, not narrower. The evidence is laid out in the gap analysis.

## Confirmed findings (21)

Each finding: **title — source document.** Short verified evidence with real figures follows.

**Valuation**

**1. P/TBV-vs-ROTE regression is a standard relative-valuation workhorse; ROTE is the load-bearing derived field — MD&A non-GAAP reconciliation (ROTE / adjusted ROTCE) + statement notes (goodwill/intangibles for tangible common equity) + market price.** Regress peer P/TBV on ROTCE across the six-bank set; above the line screens rich, below cheap — the empirical cousin of Justified P/TBV = (ROTE − g)/(r − g). P/TBV (not P/B) because acquisition goodwill inflates book (BMO's Bank of the West added $10.6B goodwill + $2.9B intangibles Feb 2023; TD carries large US-retail + imputed Schwab goodwill). TD defines TCE verbatim as "common shareholders' equity less goodwill… and… acquired intangibles, **net of related deferred tax liabilities**," Adjusted ROTCE a non-GAAP MD&A ratio. Big Six ~2.2x P/TBV vs ~1.6x 10-yr average (RBC May-2026 note: valuations "may have gone a little too far"). *Caveat: "the dominant framework" overstates — P/E and plain ROE run alongside; call it a standard framework, not the sole one.*

**2. Forward P/E is run on ADJUSTED ("cash") EPS, not reported IFRS EPS — MD&A non-GAAP reconciliation (adjusted EPS); forward EPS is analyst consensus (badge it).** Adjusted EPS strips "amortization of acquisition-related intangibles (excluding software)" plus specified items, so goodwill-heavy acquirers compare to a non-amortizing peer. RBC reconciles reported→adjusted in the "Key performance and non-GAAP measures" MD&A section. Street tracks the adjusted figure (CIBC Q2 2026 adjusted diluted EPS $2.54 vs consensus ~$1.78). RBC-analyst median forward target multiple 14.0x; group ~17.8x trailing vs 13.3x 3-yr average. **Two-thirds of a bank P/E card has no filed receipt** — forward EPS is consensus, and even trailing "E" is a non-GAAP MD&A figure, not the income-statement line: exactly the page-level-trust trap.

**3. The SFI package — not the annual report — is the primary source for segment detail and the SOTP build — Supplementary Financial Information package (primary, PDF + Excel); audited IFRS 8 segment note (coarser backstop); segment multiples are the analyst's own.** SOTP assigns a higher multiple to stable Wealth/P&C earnings, a lower one to volatile Capital Markets, and sums. Verified head-to-head on RBC Q2 2026: the SFI's per-segment pages carry segment ROE/RORC (Personal Banking ROE 25.4%, Commercial 17.4%, Capital Markets 14.8%), PCL performing/impaired split, revenue-by-sub-business, NIM, and attributed/risk capital across 9-quarter trend columns; the audited Note 13 shows only three period columns, total PCL, no ROE, no sub-business split. *Precision: bare segment net income and revenue ARE in the audited note too; the SFI-exclusive items are segment ROE, the PCL split, sub-business revenue, NIM, capital, and the trend series.*

**4. CET1 headroom above OSFI's requirement is a load-bearing valuation input — it funds the buyback/dividend-growth story — SFI regulatory-capital pages + standalone Pillar 3 report; OSFI CAR Guideline sets the threshold.** Excess capital above requirement is the direct catalyst behind the group's premium multiple. RBC Q4 2025: SFI prints CET1 13.5%, Tier 1 15.1%, Total 16.8%, leverage 4.4%; Pillar 3 KM1 discloses "CET1 available after meeting minimum requirements" = 5.4% — headroom is an *explicitly published* metric. None of CET1/Tier1/RWA/leverage/TLAC is a face-statement line. *Temporal note: the 11.5% target / 3.5% DSB was correct for FY2025; OSFI lowered the DSB to 3.0% and the target to 11.0% effective June 2026.*

**5. Dividend yield + payout ratio are a valuation pillar unique to the Canadian bank thesis; the 40–50% payout target is MD&A-only — MD&A capital-management/glossary (target range + payout ratio); earnings release + statement of changes in equity (declared DPS); forward growth is an analyst estimate.** Big Six are income compounders, so analysts weight payout more than for US banks. RBC "At a Glance Q2/2026": "payout ratio of 42%; target… 40% to 50%." TD FY2025 discloses the payout both ways (reported 36.2% FY / adjusted 50.0% FY) with the target tied to the "Glossary of the 2025 MD&A." A low payout vs range reads as dividend-growth runway and downside protection. *Precision: the ratio's basis varies by issuer — RBC frames payout on reported net income to common; TD on adjusted earnings — so a payout card must not assume "adjusted" universally.*

**6. The structural premium to US banks (discount to Australian banks) is qualitative and un-back-solvable from statements — analyst notes + MD&A risk-and-strategy narrative; supporting credit evidence in SFI credit-quality pages + loan/ACL notes.** Drivers: a five-bank oligopoly holding ~85% of deposits (pricing power), CMHC-insured/recourse mortgages suppressing credit losses (CMHC ~$488B insurance exposure), and a high-payout dividend culture. Rosenberg Research confirms the specific sub-claim — elevated "relative to U.S. and European banks, but… cheaper compared with banks in Australia." The premium is the whole question a Big Six valuation must answer, and its justification **cannot be back-solved from the numbers** — it lives in prose. *Caveats: "full-recourse" is provincial (Alberta/Saskatchewan have non-recourse features on insured mortgages); DDM/NIM/deposit-beta are universal tools, so the US-vs-Canada toolkit split is emphasis, not a methodology wall.*

**Margin / revenue**

**7. All-bank NIM lives in the SFI; segment NIM lives in the MD&A; both are non-GAAP — SFI (all-bank NIM + variants) and MD&A (segment NIM + the non-GAAP denominator definition).** NIM is nowhere in the audited statements. RBC Q4 2025 SFI p4 headline: "Net interest margin (average earning assets, net) 1.62%," plus a variant "excluding Trading Assets… and Insurance Assets 2.12%." TD Q1 2026 MD&A: Canadian P&C segment NIM 2.83% with narrative; footnote flags "average interest-earning assets… is a non-GAAP financial measure." **Comparing an all-bank NIM to a peer's segment NIM is a category error**, and the "net"/"excl. trading & insurance" variants mean two banks' headline NIM may not be like-for-like without reading the definition.

**8. Average earning assets — the NIM denominator — is an MD&A/SFI construct, absent from the audited balance sheet (which is point-in-time only) — MD&A "average balance sheet and interest rate" table + SFI average balances.** Verified on CIBC FY2023 MD&A: heading chain "MD&A > Supplementary annual financial information > Average balance sheet, net interest income and margin," columns "Average balance | Interest | Average rate"; "Net interest margin on average interest-earning assets 1.49%"; "average balances… weighted average of daily closing balances." The audited balance sheet is "as at October 31" — no average column. SFI convention (RBC/Scotia/NBC): **average loans net of ACL, all other averages gross of ACL**. **You cannot recompute or verify a bank's reported NIM from the audited statements** — the denominator exists only in MD&A/SFI, and the net-of-ACL convention must be respected or NIM is off.

**9. NII sensitivity to ±100 bps rate shocks is an MD&A Risk Management disclosure (EVE + 12-month NII), cross-referenced into the audited statements via IFRS 7 shading — MD&A Market/structural interest-rate risk section; the shaded table is an "integral part" of the audited statements under IFRS 7.** RBC FY2025 MD&A reports the before-tax impact of an immediate sustained ±100 bps shock on projected **EVE** (a value sensitivity: +100 bps → −$2,648M) and **12-month NII** (an earnings sensitivity: −100 bps → −$373M), "assuming no subsequent hedging." RBC's shaded IRR table "represent[s] an integral part of our 2025 Annual Consolidated Financial Statements" (CIBC uses the identical convention). **EVE ≠ 12-month NII** (value vs earnings) — some banks lead with one, some the other; these are modeled forward-looking outputs, not booked actuals.

**10. Trading revenue is split across two income-statement lines and only reassembled as "total trading revenue" in an MD&A table — audited income statement (the two component lines) + MD&A "Additional trading information" table (reconciliation + by-product + teb).** Under IFRS, part sits in NII (interest/dividends on trading positions) and part in non-interest income. RBC FY2024: NII component $1,742M + non-interest-income "Trading revenue" $2,327M = total $4,069M — a sum that **never appears as a statement line**. The MD&A table also carries the by-product split (interest rate & credit $2,371M, equities $817M, FX & commodities $881M) and the taxable-equivalent-basis (teb) gross-up. **Reading only the non-interest-income line understates true trading revenue by the interest component**, which can be large or negative.

**11. Segment revenue and net income appear in ALL THREE documents — audited IFRS 8 note, MD&A, SFI — and segment definitions change over time — audited segment note (authoritative, IFRS 8, RBC Note 26/27); MD&A Business Segment Review (narrative, often teb); SFI (deep quarterly detail).** Verified on RBC 2024 AR: "Note 26 Results by business segment" (pp.238-239), inside the auditor-covered notes, presents total revenue 57,344 and per-segment net income for Personal Banking, Commercial Banking, Wealth Management, Insurance, Capital Markets, Corporate Support. Note 26 verbatim: "Effective the fourth quarter of 2024, the Personal & Commercial Banking segment became two standalone business segments… Comparative results have been revised." **A 2024 re-cut means naive YoY "Personal Banking" comparisons span two definitions — a silent break a diff tool must detect.** *Correction: RBC has no "US/International" segment — Canada/US/International are geographic columns, and Corporate Support is the sixth line.*

**Credit**

**12. Headline PCL is a single audited line; the performing (Stage 1+2) vs impaired (Stage 3) split lives in the SFI and MD&A (and the audited allowance note) — audited income statement (aggregate PCL) + SFI "Provision for Credit Losses" page + MD&A credit-quality section; stage-level PCL also in the audited allowance reconciliation note.** RBC 2024: face income statement shows one line, "Provision for credit losses 3,232." The SFI breaks it into PCL on performing loans vs impaired loans ("PCL on impaired assets represents Stage 3 PCL"). **The performing-provision swing (Stage 1+2 = forward-looking macro overlay) vs the impaired run-rate (Stage 3 = actual deterioration) is the entire analytical story, and it is not on the face statement.** *The split is ALSO in the audited Notes 4/5 allowance reconciliation — the claim's own source attribution acknowledges this, so it is partly a Bucket-A note item.*

**13. PCL ratios in basis points of average loans are non-GAAP — SFI credit-quality-ratios section + MD&A non-GAAP/glossary; absent from audited statements.** RBC states PCL ratios "do not have standardized meanings under GAAP"; Q4 2025 discloses "PCL on impaired loans ratio was 37 bps," "PCL on loans… 0.39%," each footnoted to "the Glossary section of our… MD&A dated December 2, 2025." BMO FY2025: "Total provision… 63 basis points." **The bps PCL ratio is the primary cross-bank comparability metric** (34 bps vs a peer's 45 bps) — and because it is issuer-defined and denominator-dependent (average, net vs gross), it must be **badged as issuer-defined non-GAAP** under the page-level trust rule. *Precision: under NI 52-112 it is strictly a "supplementary financial measure," disclosed inside the same MD&A non-GAAP framework.*

**14. ACL coverage: the dollar allowance nets into the audited balance sheet, but the Stage 1+2 vs Stage 3 split and coverage ratios are in the SFI — audited balance sheet (netted) + audited allowance-reconciliation note (dollars); SFI "Allowance for Credit Losses" + "Credit Quality Ratios" pages (stage split + coverage %).** Verified verbatim on RBC Q4 2023 SFI (page titles identical in Q2 2025): "ACL on performing loans (Stage 1 and 2)" 4,200; "ACL on impaired loans (Stage 3)" 1,148; "Total ACL" 5,366; and in the coverage block, "ACL on loans… as a % of Total loans and acceptances" 0.61% and "ACL against impaired loans (Stage 3) as a % of GIL" **31.00%**. **Reserve adequacy (total ACL/loans building or releasing; Stage 3 ACL/GIL) is the classic pre-loss red flag, and the decisive coverage percentages are SFI-only.**

**15. Gross impaired loans (GIL) dollars sit in the audited impaired-loans note; the GIL ratio and portfolio/sector/geography breakdown are SFI — audited "Loans and allowance" note (GIL dollars, product + often geography) + SFI "Gross Impaired Loans" and "Credit Quality Ratios" pages (ratio + industry-sector granularity).** Verified: Scotiabank FY2024 Note 14(d) discloses total gross impaired loans $6,739M by product and geography; RBC Q4 2025 SFI carries "GIL by portfolio and sector" (wholesale by industry: oil and gas, automotive, agriculture…) and "GIL as a % of Related loans" 0.83% split retail/wholesale/geography. **The concentration that drives Stage 3 provisions (e.g. CRE) shows up only at SFI industry-sector granularity.** *Precision: the SFI-exclusive items are the industry-sector cut and the ratio; product + geography can appear in the audited note and the MD&A carries detailed GIL trend tables.*

**16. Write-offs are a reconciling line in the audited IFRS 9 allowance note; new impaired formations are an SFI/MD&A continuity disclosure — audited allowance reconciliation (net write-offs) + SFI/MD&A GIL continuity ("loans classified as impaired during the period"); no "formations" line on the face statements.** RBC FY2024 allowance roll-forward: opening 5,348 → PCL 3,247 → net write-offs (2,079) → closing 6,352. BMO's "Changes in Gross Impaired Loans" continuity carries "loans classified as impaired during the year" $4,047M (FY2023). **The gap between formations (feeding the future Stage 3 pipeline) and write-offs (draining Stage 3 ACL) signals whether impaired inventory is building** — write-offs are audited, formations are not, so FinLens must not treat "formations" as an audited figure.

**Capital**

**17. CET1/Tier 1/Total capital ratios appear in three places at three depths — MD&A (headline), audited capital-management note (IAS 1 compliance + summary), Pillar 3 (full composition) — MD&A Capital Management + audited Capital Management note + standalone Pillar 3 CC1/CC2 templates.** Verified on RBC Q1 2026: MD&A prints the OSFI-target-vs-actual table (CET1 13.7%) + "in compliance with all… OSFI" statement; the statement note repeats CET1 capital $100,415M / RWA $734,693M / 13.7% (aggregates only); Pillar 3 "CC1: Composition of Capital" gives the line-by-line build (retained earnings 99,023; goodwill deduction 19,101; intangibles 5,783; DTA 370; DB pension 2,977…) + a CC2 reconciliation to the balance sheet. **The number is the same everywhere; the receipt for HOW it was built exists only in the Pillar 3.** *Precision: Q1 is interim/unaudited — the *audited* capital-management note exists only at the Oct-31 year-end.*

**18. Total RWA is in the MD&A and the audited note, but RWA density and the credit/market/operational breakdown are Pillar 3-only — MD&A + audited capital note (total RWA, as ratio denominator); Pillar 3 templates OV1 (risk-type split), RWA1 (by exposure class), CR8 (RWA flow).** Verified: CIBC FY2025 audited note prints one line "Total RWA 357,803"; RBC Q4 2025 Pillar 3 OV1 decomposes Total RWA 730,225 into credit 496,113 (SA 122,661 / F-IRB 109,567 / A-IRB 263,885), CCR 28,382, CVA 19,911, market 41,506, operational 98,413, with CR8 the RWA movement statement. **RWA density (RWA ÷ total assets) is never printed — analysts derive it, and a falling density can flag model optimization rather than genuine de-risking.** *Major correction: Canadian D-SIB density is ~30–35% (RBC FY2025 730,225/2,325,006 = 31.4%; CIBC 32.0%), NOT the 55–75% the source claim asserted — that band is US-bank territory. Also MR2 is absent when a bank uses standardized market risk (RBC files MR1).*

**19. Leverage ratio: headline in the MD&A, full LR1/LR2 exposure reconciliation in Pillar 3, plus a distinct TLAC-leverage sibling — MD&A Capital Management (Tier 1 ÷ leverage exposure, ~4.2–4.4%) + Pillar 3 / Supplemental Regulatory Disclosure (LR1 reconciliation, LR2 component build); OSFI TLAC guideline defines a TLAC leverage ratio on the same denominator.** Exposure = on-balance + derivatives + SFTs + off-balance-sheet items. RBC calls the document "Pillar 3 Report"; TD calls it "Supplemental Regulatory Disclosure" — same templates. **The non-risk-weighted leverage backstop can bind before CET1 for low-density trading/derivatives-heavy books, so it must be read alongside CET1 — but its explanatory detail is in a different document than the ratio.**

**20. LCR and NSFR are liquidity metrics — MD&A Liquidity/Funding section + Pillar 3 (LIQ1/LIQ2), essentially never in the audited statement notes — MD&A Liquidity and Funding Risk + standalone Pillar 3 LIQ1 (LCR)/LIQ2 (NSFR); OSFI regulatory returns, externally auditor-assured (D-SIBs, from FY2025) via the OSFI channel, NOT the financial-statement audit.** RBC 2024 AR reports LCR as "the average for the three months ended" (Q4 128%). OSFI's 2022 Assurance Guideline puts LCR/NSFR returns in scope for external-auditor opinion delivered to OSFI within 90 days of year-end. **Capital adequacy (CET1) and liquidity adequacy (LCR/NSFR) are separate regulatory regimes in separate sections — a tool scoping "bank regulatory metrics" to the capital note systematically misses liquidity, the most acutely fatal risk in a run (SVB 2023).** *Precision: OSFI's NSFR guideline permits notes placement; Big Six practice keeps them in MD&A/Pillar 3.*

**21. TLAC has NO fixed home — OSFI explicitly lets each D-SIB choose where to disclose it — MD&A OR notes OR SFI OR standalone Pillar 3 (issuer's choice, per the TLAC Disclosure Requirements Guideline); the per-bank minimum is set in that bank's OSFI TLAC Order.** OSFI verbatim: "D-SIBs may choose where to provide the disclosures… (e.g. management discussion and analysis, financial statement notes, supplemental information or standalone Pillar 3 report)." The two required ratios (Template KM2: TLAC/RWA and TLAC leverage) landed in RBC's MD&A (29.3% / 8.3% at Oct-31-2024) but in CIBC's combined Pillar 3 + SRC document. Named per-bank Orders exist ("Royal Bank of Canada – Order Providing for Minimum TLAC," CIBC likewise), floor 21.5% risk-based / 6.75% leverage. **This is the single most important location caveat for automation: a fixed-location scraper will miss TLAC on some banks — FinLens must search across MD&A, notes, SFI, and Pillar 3.**

## Corrections — refuted claims (3)

Three plausible-sounding claims were **refuted** on their source-document attribution and are corrected here. (No claims landed unverified.) **Notably, two of the three refutations *improve* FinLens's opportunity — they move high-value data out of an assumed hard-to-reach class and into the audited statements/notes FinLens already ingests.**

**C1. "Non-interest income is audited in total but only disaggregated by product in a note." — REFUTED (the mix is on the FACE of the audited income statement).** Verified against CIBC FY2025 audited Consolidated Statement of Income (p.110): the full non-interest-income product mix is itemized **directly on the face** — Underwriting and advisory fees 915; Deposit and payment fees 996; Credit fees 1,015; Card fees 402; Investment management and custodial fees 2,241; Mutual fund fees 2,019; Income from insurance activities, net 317; Commissions on securities 554; FVTPL gains 4,022; FX other than trading 369; Other 411; Total non-interest income 13,364 — sitting above Net interest income 15,769. RBC's audited income statement follows the identical pattern. **Correction:** the fee/net-interest-income durability mix is a ratio computable **directly from audited face-of-statement line items** — no disaggregation note required. The IFRS-15 revenue note and MD&A add BY-SEGMENT color only. **This is the single cheapest, highest-value Bucket-A win in banking.**

**C2. "IFRS 9 stage-migration is in the notes, with TWO transfer reconciliations (ECL allowance AND gross carrying amount)." — REFUTED (single reconciliation, not two; audited-note location confirmed).** Verified on RBC 2024 AR Note 5 (p.188) and TD 2024 AR: the IFRS 7.35H reconciliation with explicit "Transfers to stage 1/2/3," "Originations," "Maturities," "Changes in risk parameters," "Write-offs," "Recoveries" across Stage 1/2/3 columns is presented for the **ECL allowance only**. The gross carrying amount is disclosed separately by stage as a static "Credit risk exposure by internal risk rating" table (IFRS 7.35M) — **no transfer/movement lines**. **Correction:** there is one transfer matrix (the allowance), not two. **The core value stands and improves the plan:** the stage-migration reconciliation — the earliest quantified deterioration signal, preceding Stage 3 formations by quarters — is in the **audited notes**, i.e. receipted/validated data, distinct from the non-GAAP SFI ratios that need trust badging. FinLens should extract it as a Bucket-A notes target.

**C3. "OSFI sets the capital requirement externally; the requirement itself is not in the filing." — REFUTED (the requirement IS disclosed in the bank's own MD&A + Pillar 3).** All numbers/dates confirm (DSB 3.5%→3.0%, target CET1 11.5%→11.0% effective June-19-2026; composition 4.5% + 2.5% + 1.0% D-SIB + DSB), and OSFI is indeed the body that *sets* the DSB. But RBC's MD&A/Pillar 3 reproduce the requirement verbatim: "OSFI's minimum… requirements, including the D-SIB 1.0% surcharge and its DSB, are: 11.5%, 13.0% and 15.0% for CET1, Tier 1 and Total capital." **Correction:** the DSB/target CET1 is **set by OSFI** (authoritative source: OSFI DSB decisions + CAR Guideline) **and disclosed inside the bank's own MD&A and Pillar 3**, which cite OSFI as the setter. Drop "not in the filing" — an analyst reads the applicable target CET1 and DSB level directly in the bank's disclosures, so FinLens can source the hurdle from the same documents as the actual ratio.

### Bucket C — SFI / Pillar 3-gated (new document class; deep moat; O&G AIF / mining NI 43-101 analog — but standardized & machine-readable)

**Uncovered; the deep-moat lift.** This is banking's structural equivalent of the O&G AIF / mining technical report — **but broader** (two regulatory documents, SFI + Pillar 3) **and cheaper to parse**: the Basel Pillar 3 templates (KM1, CC1/CC2, OV1, RWA1, CR8, LR1/LR2, LIQ1/LIQ2, KM2/TLAC) are **standardized and identical across the Big Six**, and RBC posts its SFI in **Excel** as well as PDF — unlike the bespoke free-text NI 43-101, this is form-parsing, not free-text NLP. ## What FinLens already covers well (from statements — confirmed adequate)

FinLens maps the audited spine of a bank honestly, and the balance-sheet identity ties exactly. On the RY_FY2025 fixture (RBC, the first filer human-verified against the source PDF for NII/revenue/PCL/net_income, ground-truthed by Riley 2026-06-17): **8 fields map** — `net_interest_income` 33,000 and `provision_for_credit_losses` 4,362 from the `financial_sector` profile (the two bank-distinctive fields, PCL with the CB-018 parenthetical alias), plus `revenue` 66,605, `pretax_income` 25,651 (CB-023), `net_income` 20,369 (signed, consolidated incl. NCI, CB-011/020), `total_assets` 2,325,006, `total_liabilities` 2,185,855 (CB-013), and `total_equity` 139,151 (grand-total incl. NCI, CB-013) from the default profile. The identity closes (2,185,855 + 139,151 = 2,325,006). The suppression doctrine is also correct and deliberate: **Debt/Equity is suppressed** for `financial_sector` (CB-022 — deposits and policy liabilities make D/E a different concept, honest N/A) and **EBIT/EBITDA/interest-cover are gated off** (CB-023 — interest is core to a bank, not a financing add-back; `pretax_income` still maps, only the derived metrics are withheld). So FinLens holds the audited anchor lines and the identity, and correctly *withholds* the corporate ratios that would mislead on a bank — exactly the right statements-layer behavior. What it does not yet do is map the bank-distinctive *statement and note* lines (fee mix, interest expense, non-interest expense, loans/deposits/ACL, the stage-migration and segment notes) that Bucket A shows are already in the documents it ingests.

## Honest caveats (recorded verbatim from the run)

- **No paywalled bank research was sourced.** The "standard Big Six analyst metric set" is reconstructed from public FIG/IB valuation references, OSFI regulatory texts, the Basel Pillar 3 framework, IFRS/IAS standards, and issuer primary filings — not from an actual RBC/TD/BMO/Scotia FIG note. The valuation-methodology weighting question (P/TBV-regression vs forward P/E vs SOTP vs DDM as the *lead* anchor) is answered only indirectly.
- **SEC EDGAR HTML repeatedly 403'd direct fetch.** Several primary-filing details (RBC 40-F/6-K exhibits, SFI and Pillar 3 image-encoded PDFs) were recovered via issuer-hosted PDFs (rbc.com/investor-relations, td.com) and search-engine extraction of the same documents. Where an SFI page resisted text extraction (RBC's is partly image-encoded), page *titles* and *structure* were confirmed but a few exact line strings (e.g. the literal "CREDIT QUALITY RATIOS" heading) rest on cross-period/cross-issuer corroboration rather than byte-level fetch of that one page.
- **Regulatory thresholds are time-varying.** The 11.5% target CET1 / 3.5% DSB is correct for FY2025; OSFI lowered the DSB to 3.0% and the target to 11.0% effective June-19-2026. Valuation multiples (P/TBV ~2.2x, P/E ~17.8x, 14x forward "fair") are current-environment central tendencies and will move with the cycle.
- **RWA-density magnitude was corrected during the run** — a source claim of 55–75% Canadian universal-bank density was refuted; the real D-SIB figure is ~30–35% (RBC 31.4%, CIBC 32.0%), back-solved from the actual filings. This is exactly the "back-solve the displayed number" verification move from the page-level-trust principle, applied to a research claim.
- **0 claims landed unverified** (21 confirmed, 3 refuted, 0 unverified) — a cleaner run than the O&G sweep (which had 13 rate-limited-unverified), reflecting that Big Six disclosures are unusually well-structured and cross-corroborated.
- **Ranking is judgment-based.** The Bucket A/B/C prioritization is CC's read of build economics, not a measured ROI.
- **Single fixture.** Bank coverage is machine-verified on RBC (RY_FY2025) only — n=1. A TD or CIBC fixture would pin `financial_sector` beyond one filer (TD's segment/adjusted-basis presentation differs materially from RBC's).

## Implications for the build order (CC's read, for Riley's ratification)

Banking tracks the ratified O&G and mining sequences and **shares their infrastructure at every step** — but the ordering is different because banking's cheap-win surface is unusually deep, and its deep-moat surface is standardized.

1. **Statement/notes cheap wins now (Bucket A) — bigger and higher-value than in O&G/mining.** The two refutations moved the crown-jewel disclosures into documents FinLens already ingests. In rough order: (a) **map the non-interest-income product mix** — the fee/spread durability split, an *audited face line* today unmapped (correction C1); (b) **map `interest_expense` (bank "– …"-suffixed form) and `non_interest_expense`** — these two lines unlock the NIM numerator and the **efficiency ratio**, the flagship bank metric, from data already in the fixture; (c) **map loans / deposits / allowance for loan losses** for loan-to-deposit and coverage; (d) **run the shipped CB-034 note extractor on the audited IFRS 9 stage-migration reconciliation** (correction C2) — the earliest deterioration signal, receipted, the exact reuse case the extractor was built for but never exercised on a bank; extend the same pattern to the segment (IFRS 8) note and the write-offs roll-forward; (e) **add a `financial_sector` red-flag profile** (CB-019 Q5 residual risk); (f) **clean up the `total_revenue_bank` dead alias.** None require a new fetcher.

2. **MD&A fetcher — the same build as O&G's and mining's next step, serving all three sectors.** It converts Bucket B from uncovered to covered: ROTE/adjusted ROTCE (the P/TBV-regression field), adjusted EPS, payout target range, segment NIM, the average-balance-sheet table (the NIM denominator), NII rate sensitivity, and the trading-revenue reassembly. **Priority: immediately after the Bucket A wins, exactly as ratified for O&G and mining** — one MD&A fetcher, three sectors.

3. **Regulatory-disclosure layer (Bucket C) — the deep moat, but cheaper here than the reserve-report class.** SFI + Pillar 3 carry CET1 headroom, the capital composition, RWA breakdown/density, leverage reconciliation, LCR/NSFR, TLAC, all-bank NIM, and the full SFI credit-quality suite (PCL split, bps ratios, ACL coverage, GIL by sector, formations). Because the **Basel Pillar 3 templates are standardized and identical across the Big Six** and RBC posts SFI in **Excel**, this is *form-parsing*, not the bespoke free-text NLP the NI 43-101 technical report demands — so the banking deep-moat lift is likely **cheaper per unit of coverage** than the mining/O&G reserves layer. Treat "regulatory-disclosure document" as one new class with a banking (Pillar 3/SFI) branch alongside the reserve-report class's mining (43-101) and O&G (51-101) branches. **Badge every SFI/Pillar 3 non-GAAP ratio** (PCL bps, RWA density, NIM variants) under Trust-Is-Page-Level.

4. **The Big Six are a natural peer set — the banking analog of the WCSB peer group.** A homogeneous, six-name universe on identical Basel templates makes cross-bank comparison (efficiency, PCL bps, CET1 headroom, ROTE, P/TBV) a corpus-native strength: store one bank's SFI and every YoY/peer delta comes for free — the same flywheel argument as WCSB peers in O&G, but with *more* standardization to exploit.

5. **Zero-cost disclosure captions in parallel.** Add: a **non-GAAP badge on adjusted EPS / ROTE / PCL-bps / NIM** (issuer-defined, denominator-dependent); a **consensus badge on forward EPS** (no filed source — the page-level-trust trap that a bank P/E card walks straight into); a **segment-recut warning** on YoY segment comparisons (finding 11); and a **TLAC/liquidity "may be absent from notes — sourced from Pillar 3" caption**. These need no fetcher and directly serve Trust-Is-Page-Level.

**Shared-infrastructure summary:** the **MD&A fetcher** (one build, now three sectors), the **CB-034 note extractor** (shipped, reusable for banking's stage-migration / segment / write-offs / capital-composition notes), a generalized **regulatory-disclosure document class** (Pillar 3/SFI ∥ NI 43-101 ∥ NI 51-101), the **Big-Six-as-WCSB peer set**, and **non-GAAP/consensus badging**. Banking's distinctive wrinkle versus O&G/mining runs *in FinLens's favor*: its highest-value analytics (fee mix, stage migration, PCL, segment results) are **already inside the audited statements and notes FinLens ingests** — the cheap-win Bucket A is deeper here than in any prior sector, and it should ship first.
