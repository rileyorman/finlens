FinLens Operating Guide
Source-Traced Fundamental Analysis · every tile, metric & chart explained
July 2026
dev branch

FinLens turns a filing — an uploaded PDF, an EDGAR fetch, or a SEDAR+ document — into an investment analysis workspace: statements, a verdict, health metrics, valuation tools, sector economics, and portfolio views. Its founding principle is honest numbers or no numbers: when FinLens can't compute something safely, it shows a dash and tells you why, never a confident wrong figure.

Section 1

The 60-second tour

The sidebar has five pages. Single Analysis is home: load a filing, read the verdict, work the tabs. Portfolio Manager rolls your analyzed companies into one risk view. Filing Comparison diffs two filings from the same company year-over-year. Stock Tracker is live market data with no upload needed. Education explains every concept — the "Learn more →" links throughout the app land there.

The typical loop: load a filing → the verdict hero tells you signal, quality, and thesis at a glance → Statements for the numbers → Dashboard for health, valuation, and the manager frameworks → Economics if it's a miner or oil & gas producer → export to Excel or a Word memo → it's saved in the Filing Library for free reloads.

Under the hood: a fast model (Haiku) extracts the statements; a reasoning model (Sonnet) writes the analysis — that split is what keeps a full analysis near eight cents. Model choices live in config.py.

Section 3

Reading the verdict

Every loaded analysis opens with the verdict hero — a banner tinted by the investment signal, carrying the signal badge, the quality chip, the filing period and currency, and the full thesis paragraph.

As it appears in the app
INVESTIGATE Quality 33 / 100 · 3 of 5 metrics 2024-12-31 · CAD

CNQ generated CAD 13.4B in operating cash flow in 2024 against CAD 7.1B returned to shareholders…

The signal

Claude's overall read of the filing, in three levels:

SignalMeaning
PASSFundamentals support a closer look as an investment candidate.
INVESTIGATEMixed picture — specific questions need answers before conviction either way.
DECLINEThe filing shows disqualifying problems for this framework.

The Quality score

A 0–100 score built from five financial-health checks — the same five graded Green/Amber/Red in the Dashboard's Financial Health panel:

#MetricWhat it measuresThe question it answers
1LiquidityCurrent ratio (current assets ÷ current liabilities)Can it pay its bills this year?
2LeverageNet debt ÷ EBITDAHow many years of earnings to clear the debt?
3Revenue growthYear-over-year revenue changeIs the top line growing?
4FCF conversionFree cash flow ÷ net incomeDo reported profits become real cash?
5Interest coverageOperating income ÷ interest expenseHow comfortably does it service its debt?

Each available metric earns Green = 20, Amber = 10, Red = 0 points, and the total is rescaled to what was actually measurable — the chip always states its basis ("3 of 5 metrics"). Missing data is not poor quality: a sparse quarterly filing scores over what it shows, not against what it lacks. If nothing is available the chip simply doesn't appear.

Worked example: a filing with one Green (20), one Amber (10), one Red (0) and two metrics unavailable earns 30 of a possible 60 → Quality 50 / 100 · 3 of 5 metrics. The exact Green/Amber/Red cutoffs for each metric are in the reference tables, and the Financial Health panel on the Dashboard shows you exactly which grade each metric received.

Integrity & mapping chips

Above the company name
⚑ revenue_growth_sanity SEDAR+ mapping · 6 of 54 lines mapped to canonical fields

Integrity flags are the Data Integrity Engine's audit trail — sanity checks and accounting identities that ran against this filing. A flag means "verify this figure against source," not "the app is broken." The mapping chip (Canadian filings) reports how many statement lines resolved to canonical fields; the unmapped line items expander lists the rest verbatim. Unmapped is honest, not hidden.

The 📈 Track live in Stock Tracker button under the hero hands the ticker to Stock Tracker with data already fetched.

Section 4

Statements tab

  • Income Statement / Balance Sheet / Cash Flow render as full-width tabs, two fiscal years side by side, formatted in the filing's own scale (K/M/B/T).
  • Scenario Simulator (expander): stress revenue and margins and watch distress score, scenario EBIT, and implied P/E respond, with a Claude narrative of the outcome.
  • Bull / Bear Case (expander): the top three points each way.
  • Downloads: a formatted Excel workbook (statements, summary metrics, RAG sheet) and a Word investment memo.
Section 5

Dashboard tab

Header cards — Quality · Beta · Margin of Safety

Quality Score repeats the hero's score with its basis. Beta (5-year monthly) arrives with market data — a dash means market data isn't loaded yet. Margin of Safety fills in after you run the DCF below: intrinsic value per share and the % gap to price (green > 20% undervalued, amber ≥ 0, red overvalued).

DCF Calculator

Four inputs — WACC (default 10%), terminal growth (3%), revenue growth (5%), forecast period (10 years) — produce intrinsic value per share and the margin of safety, which then feeds the header card and the Valuation Snapshot.

Peer Comparison

Pick a sector bucket (auto-suggested from the filing's own sector text — a caption tells you what was picked and why), edit the comma-separated peer list to any tickers you like (Canadian names welcome), and Load. The table shows P/E, EV/EBITDA, P/B, P/S, revenue growth, and gross margin against the sector median: green is meaningfully better than median, red meaningfully worse, and gray means within 10% of the median — a hairline difference is not a verdict. Peer figures are trailing-twelve-month market data via yfinance, not filing-period numbers.

Framework Perspectives — Dalio · Lynch · Marks

Three managers' lenses on the same filing: Dalio (purple) places the company in its debt/productivity cycle; Lynch (green) assigns a category — Fast Grower, Stalwart, Cyclical, Turnaround, Asset Play, Slow Grower — with the story; Marks (amber) asks the second-level question the crowd isn't asking.

Key Metrics row

Revenue (with YoY change), gross margin, EBITDA margin, net income (with net margin), and free cash flow — all pulled from the mapped statements.

Two-Year Performance charts

  • Grouped bars: revenue, gross profit, operating income, net income, and operating cash flow — prior year in teal, current in blue, one shared unit on the axis.
  • Margins: gross / operating / net margin, both years, in percent.
  • Cash-flow waterfall — adaptive: when the filing details at least two of capex / buybacks / dividends and they total ≥ 10% of operating cash flow, you get the allocation story (OCF → outflows → retained cash). Otherwise it falls back to the always-available bridge: Operating → Investing → Financing → net change in cash, which closes exactly for any filing.

Financial Health (RAG) & Capital Allocation

The five health checks with their values and Green/Amber/Red chips (thresholds in the reference tables), beside Claude's read of buybacks, dividends, M&A, and the cash position.

Dalio Stress Scenarios & Market Sensitivity

Interest coverage recomputed under four shocks — EBIT −30%, EBIT −60%, revenue −20% with margin compression, and a credit seizure — each graded SAFE (≥3.0×), WATCH (≥1.5×), STRESS (≥1.0×), or DISTRESS. Beside it, beta with a plain-language interpretation (defensive / market-neutral / cyclical / high-beta).

Variant Perception & Valuation Snapshot

What consensus believes versus what the data suggests, and the quick multiples: P/E, EV/EBITDA, EV/Revenue, and your DCF margin of safety.

Framework-Attributed Cases & MD&A Sentiment

Bull and bear cases with each point attributed to the framework that generated it (green cards bull, red cards bear). For uploaded documents with MD&A prose, a sentiment read (Positive / Cautious / Negative) with the language evidence.

Section 6

Economics tab — resource filers

Appears with live data only when the mapper resolves a mining or oil & gas sector profile; other filers see an explanatory note. Everything here is filing fundamentals crossed with free yfinance market data — no extra API cost.

Tier 1 cards

CardWhat it isHonest-skip behavior
Earnings YieldNet income ÷ market cap — the value investor's inverted P/E.Suppressed with a note whenever filing currency ≠ listing currency. FinLens first tries the sibling listing (SU → SU.TO for a CAD filing; AEM.TO → AEM for a USD filer) so dual-listed names usually resolve; it never silently converts FX.

Commodity SpotLatest price via the commodity proxy (see proxy table).Names its proxy and role (primary/fallback).
Commodity LinkageCorrelation (r) and beta (β) of one year of daily returns, equity vs. commodity. r near 1 = the stock trades like the commodity; β above 1 = it amplifies commodity moves (operational leverage). Agnico: r = 0.76, β = 1.21×.Skips below 20 overlapping trading days.
Cheap company?Earnings yield tested against an 8% threshold, drawn as a bar on a 0–16% scale with the threshold as the amber mid-tick — you see how far from cheap, not just a verdict.Carries the reason when earnings yield is unavailable.

The three charts

  • Commodity Price Trend — one year of the proxy, unit-labeled axis (US$/bbl, US$/oz…), your filing's fiscal-year-end as a dotted line, and the latest close marked so the spot card ties to the chart. WTI carries a caveat for Canadian producers (WCS differential).
  • Company vs Commodity, indexed to 100 — both series rebased a year back on one axis. Read the gap: lines together = the equity is a commodity proxy; equity above = the market is re-rating the operator beyond the commodity (Suncor 155 vs WTI 103); below = it lags its own product.
  • Cost Structure waterfall — the top line minus the mapped sector costs (mining: production costs, exploration, mine D&A; oil & gas: royalties, operating expense, DD&A) down to margin after these costs. For O&G the caption also reports the royalty burden as a share of the gross top line (CNQ: 14.1%). The end bar deliberately excludes G&A, finance and other items — it will not equal printed operating income, and says so.
Coming — Tier 2
The flagship margin-of-safety metric: AISC vs spot (miners) and netback vs price (oil & gas). Those figures live in the MD&A, not the statements, so this tier waits on the MD&A fetcher rather than fake a number.

The Data provenance & coverage expander shows exactly which ticker/listing was resolved, the commodity and proxy, the scale factor applied, and any fetch errors — the receipts for everything above.

Section 7

Portfolio Manager

  • Portfolio Summary: holdings count, signal tallies (PASS / INVESTIGATE / DECLINE), and average quality across scoreable holdings.
  • Predictive Analytics: a Financial Distress Score per holding (Altman-derived, 0 safe → 100 distressed; safe < 40, watch 40–70, distress > 70), a Momentum Scorecard (revenue/margin/cash-flow trends graded Improving or Mixed), and Concentration Risk bars for signal and sector concentration.
  • Portfolio Analytics: quality trend over fiscal years, the valuation scatter (quality vs P/E — top-left is the sweet spot), a risk/return matrix (momentum vs distress), and an earnings-quality table (FCF conversion, revenue quality).
  • Holdings table and Framework Scores: quality bars per holding, with each company's full Dalio and Marks narratives in expanders below — never truncated into table cells.
  • Positions: lot-level tracking (buys, sells, cost basis) feeds live P&L where enabled.
Section 8

Stock Tracker

  • Header: company identity, analyst consensus badge (Strong Buy → Sell), price target with upside %, analyst count, and a current-vs-target bar.
  • Price & Momentum: current price, 52-week high/low with distance, market cap; a 1-year price chart with 50-day (dotted) and 200-day (dashed) moving averages plus volume; badges for Above 50MA / Above 200MA and Golden/Death Cross; RSI-14 graded Oversold (<30) / Neutral / Overbought (>70).
  • Sub-tabs: Technicals (short interest — LOW <5% of float, ELEVATED ≤15%, HIGH above), Fundamentals (valuation multiples with plain-language notes), Earnings (next date, EPS estimate vs last actual, beat/miss), Segments, News & Sentiment, and Filings.
  • Watchlist (sidebar): one-click add, quick re-load, and a summary table.
Section 9

Filing Comparison & Education

Filing Comparison takes two filings from the same company (from the library or fresh uploads) and reports the year-over-year story: MD&A sentiment shift (Positive / Cautious / Negative), metric deltas, and theme tracking — which topics are new, which disappeared, and which changed in emphasis. Language change in the MD&A often leads the numbers.

Education is the built-in glossary and curriculum — every "Learn more →" link in the app (Quality Score, Beta, Margin of Safety, DCF, Investment Signals, Financial Statements…) lands on its explainer here.

Analyses cache to ~/.finlens with a schema version — if the data format evolves, old entries prompt a Re-Analyse instead of rendering silently wrong. Section 12

Tips & troubleshooting

  • Stray "?" in older analysis text — legacy cache damage from before the typography fix; a Re-Analyse $ regenerates clean text. New analyses are unaffected.
  • Beta / Margin of Safety showing "—" — load market data via the ticker lookup, and run the DCF for MoS.
  • Economics ratios suppressed — the currency guard found no listing matching the filing currency; the note names both currencies. Honest skip beats silent FX.
  • Dev tools (model selector, response cache controls, raw-extraction debug) — set FINLENS_DEBUG=1 in .env.
  • Ports — the always-on service runs at :8501; use :8502 for development sessions.
  • Costs — Library loads are free; only Quick Analysis, fetches, uploads, and Re-Analyse spend (~$0.08 each).
